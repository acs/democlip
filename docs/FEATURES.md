# Features

Everything DemoClip does, in full. The [README](../README.md) keeps the headline
capabilities; this is the whole list, grouped by what you are doing. The categories
are the app's main use cases — record, import, keep, restore, cutting, annotating,
export, publish, profiles and projects — with a section for the clip's own properties, for the
round trip, and for the app itself, named as [`GLOSSARY.md`](GLOSSARY.md) names them.

## Record

- **Nothing captured until you ask** — every source starts off, so the app never opens a screen picker on its own; press **Screen** or **Webcam** to start one, and Record with nothing on says so
- **Record sources in one row** — Screen, Webcam and Mic sit on the left, the record button is centred in the bar, and the sources are out while a take is being set up or records. They are independent toggles, **all off at start**: press Screen to share, and press it again to let the share go — nothing reaches for the screen after that — or Webcam, which carries the picture on its own when the screen is off. **Mic on its own records a sound-only take** — no picture at all — reviewed as a waveform where the thumbnails would be; a clip can mix sound-only takes with picture takes, and the picture takes stay what they were. The recording's duration is read off the timeline rather than a separate clock
- **Region crop** — a red box over the preview always shows what will be recorded: the whole screen by default, drag to record just part of it, click to reset; while recording the box breathes (red ↔ background)
- **Webcam** — a source button adds a mirrored bubble in the corner of the recording (disabled when no camera is present), or is **the whole recording** when the screen is off; when the device has more than one camera, a picker beside the button chooses which one
- **Microphone** — a source button (disabled when no mic) with a live level meter; its audio is **mixed with the tab/system audio** in the recording, and on its own it makes a **sound-only** clip
- **Tab / system audio** — requested on every capture (`audio: true` plus Chrome's `systemAudio: 'include'` hint, so the audio option is offered); the browser owns that checkbox, so the app says so when a share comes back silent, and **New** returns to the picker to redo it
- **Add more takes** — pressing Record after a recording appends the new one to the end of the clip; the share is kept between takes, so there is no second picker and **every take in a clip comes from the same source** (the same tab, window or screen). **A short fade either side of the join, and a dissolve across it**, soften the change from one take to the next. New starts a new clip; if a source ends, turn it back on and the next take still adds on
- **Live timeline** — the timeline is there while a take records, filling in with the picture and the sound as they are captured (drawn from the live preview and the recorded stream's own levels, never by decoding the half-written file), and it becomes the timeline you cut on when you stop
- **Audio track** — a waveform of the recording under the thumbnails, shown when the clip has sound; it is drawn at the **level the sound is heard at**, so a quiet take the **sound boost** lifts reads as tall as it will sound

## Import

- **Import a file** — press **Import** to bring a **WebM or MP4** already on your machine in as a take — a video, or a sound-only file, which is what an audio export writes: it joins the clip to cut, annotate, export and publish like a recording. Nothing is re-recorded — the file's own bytes become the take, and its own length, sound and picture are read from the file — and it is read back by the same decoder the export writes with, so the two containers an export can read are the two an import takes

## Keep

- **Your recording is not lost to a reload** — the clip is kept in the browser (IndexedDB) as it changes; the session is over at **New**, or when the clip is exported (the file written, the post published), and the kept copy goes with it

## Restore

- **It is back where you left it** — a page reload, an update, or the trip a Bluesky sign-in takes all come back to the clip, opened straight into the review

## Cutting

- **Timeline cutting** — **click twice to select a section and cut it out with the scissors** (or by clicking inside it), with an **undo** on the section to drop the selection and an **undo** in the header to put an approved cut back. **An approved cut leaves the timeline**, which closes up over it, so the timeline always shows what the export will hold — the strip, the waveform and the playhead all follow. **Drag a mark or the selected section** (whole, or by an edge) to reposition it, with the frame under it following as it moves, or **step it a frame at a time with ← / →** (Shift a second) — a click, a drag or a step **holds the frame, it does not play**, so the exact moment can be found by eye
- **Playback** — play the recorded clip back on the preview with sound (**P** or ▶), skipping the cuts

## Annotating

- **Annotations — point with an arrow, put a picture over it, or write on it** — press a kind in the **annotate options**, the column of controls on the picture under the clip's settings pencil: the **arrow** (drag across the preview to point it at the detail), the **image** (choose a file, **paste one**, or **drop one on the preview**, then drag its corners to resize — hold **Shift** to stretch it freely), or the **text** (type a line, which grows and shrinks as you drag a corner). With a stretch selected on the timeline the annotation is shown over it; with nothing selected it starts at the playhead for a couple of seconds. Any kind is shown for its own **duration** — a seconds value in its **settings**, changed after it is placed — in playback and in the export — **dissolving onto the picture at the start of its stretch and off it at the end, as a take does at a join, so it never pops in or out** — stays over the same part of the picture as the frames move, and is kept with the clip across a reload. **Click one on the picture** to pick it and drag it to move (an arrow's ends aim it, a picture's or a text's corners resize it), **click its mark on the timeline lane** to jump to it (a picked annotation's stretch is drawn the whole way along the lane), **copy and paste it** (**⌘/Ctrl+C** then **⌘/Ctrl+V**) for a new one of the same shape at the playhead, or delete it by pressing **Delete** with it picked, or by tapping the **×** that appears on it — and the **copy** beside the **×** puts another like it on the picture, to drag where it is wanted — and the **settings** control beside them changes what the annotation is made of: how long it is shown (its **duration**), its **colour** from the app's palette, and for a text its **words**, typed in the app's own dialog — a new one starts in the clip's **annotation colour** (red, unless the clip's own settings or a profile say otherwise), and changing that colours what is made next rather than what is already placed — and the header's one **undo** takes back whatever you last did, a cut or an annotation. **A cut takes its part of an annotation's stretch with it**, exactly as it takes the picture

## What a clip says about itself

- **Sound annotations — put a sound over a stretch** — press **Sound** in the **annotate options** (the row sits beside the **arrow**, **image** and **text**, and a **sound-only** clip offers it on its own) and one of the app's own sounds — a **chime**, a **gong**, a **whoosh** and more — plays over the selected stretch, **mixed with the recorded sound**, so the recording is still heard beneath it. It **draws nothing**: it is heard, not drawn, so it is never picked on the picture — it appears as a mark on the **timeline lane**, and picking that mark offers its **remove** and **settings** controls — and its **settings** are where you choose which sound it is (a **drop-down** of the app's own list, with **one ▶ to hear the chosen sound** before you keep it, and no file to load) and how long it plays (its **duration**). A sound plays only over its stretch, in the review and in every file — the WebM and MP4 downloads, a publish and a sound-only file alike — and **a cut takes its part of it**, exactly as it takes a picture's. The takes are never changed: removing the sounds gives the recording back whole, and the clip's **sound boost** keeps treating the recorded sound alone, with the added sound over it
- **Magnifier — enlarge a detail and dim the rest** — press **Magnifier** in the **annotate options** (the row sits beside the **arrow**, **image**, **text** and **sound**; a **sound-only** clip does not offer it, because there is no picture to read) and drag from the detail outward: a round **lens** appears over the picture, showing the **magnified region** — the centre of its own circle — enlarged, while a **spotlight dim** darkens the rest of the picture for the stretch of the annotation. The lens is a circle on any picture shape, and its centre and radius are kept as fractions of the picture, so it lands in the same place in the review, in every file and after a reload. Its **settings** are its **rim**'s **colour** from the app's palette and its **zoom level** — 2× unless chosen, stepped from 1.5× to 8× — plus the shared **duration**; the zoom enlarges the detail more or less, and the colour paints the rim. The lens reads the picture **beneath** it, never the app's own annotations: the layer takes one copy of the frame before any mark is drawn and hands it in, so the magnified region is the recording itself. A magnifier is picked, moved, resized from its rim's grips, copied, pasted, deleted and undone exactly like any other kind; a **cut takes its part** of its stretch, and the clip keeps it across a reload
- **A clip's own title and description** — the **settings** control in the corner of the preview (the pencil over the picture) opens what the clip says about itself: a **title** and a **description**, its own properties, kept with the clip and taken back by the one undo. Publishing fills its fields from them — a YouTube video's title and description, a Bluesky post's text and its alt — so the clip is branded once and the branding goes out with the upload, changed per publish if you want. Switch on **Add to export** and **every export — the files and a publish alike — begins with a 2-second title card**: the clip's first frame held still, with the title and description drawn over it as text and the date and the app's name under them, so a file opens by saying what it is and a published video opens with it too
- **One size for every take** — a clip's takes may come from different crops and different files, so the clip has its own **clip size**: the largest width and height among the takes, both even, so no take is scaled below its own picture. Every take is shown **fitted whole and centred** in the review and written the same way in every export, so a file never changes picture size partway. The size is derived from the takes and is **not yours to set**: the clip's settings show it, and the size a **file** is written at is the **export resolution** beside it
- **Choose the size the file is written at — the export resolution** — the **settings** control in the corner of the preview carries, beside the clip size, the size every export and publish is finally written at: **Clip size** — the default, so a file is exactly what you watched — or one of DemoClip's own levels, **2160p, 1440p, 1080p, 720p, 480p**. It is the clip size **scaled**, so the file keeps the clip's own shape: the same picture at fewer (or more) pixels, with nothing cropped, stretched or letterboxed. The encoder's bitrate follows that picture, so a smaller resolution is a **smaller file** of the same clip — and the size of that file sits **under the Export button**, moving with the resolution, so the figure is there before you export. The review and the clip size are untouched, and a **profile may fix it**, so a production writes every clip at one resolution with nothing to press
- **A logo on the head of the export, and as a watermark over the whole clip** — a clip wears a **logo** as one of its own properties: the **name** of a mark DemoClip serves itself (a file in the repository, nothing uploaded), or nothing at all, which is the ordinary case. A clip that wears one opens **every** export — the WebM and MP4 files, the file for editing and a publish — with a **title card** carrying it, above the clip's words; and a name the app does not ship is refused rather than kept, so a file is never waited on that will never draw. The same mark is worn as a **watermark** over the whole clip: an **image annotation** in the top-right corner, and the clip's **title** when it has one, half the mark's height, as a line of text **in the clip's annotation colour** under it — or on its own in the corner when the clip wears no mark. Both are scaled and never stretched, so they are on every frame of the file and can be moved or resized like any other annotation; clearing the logo takes the mark away and clearing the title the line, and either part can be taken away on its own. A **profile** may fix it, which is what makes a production environment wear the same mark every time

## Projects

- **A project is the reason, not just a folder** — the **projects pill** at the right of the toolbar (beside the profile pill) opens the projects: the one the app is in is marked, and **New project** asks for a name, a motivation and the objectives the work must achieve. Every clip made while a project is current is kept as a **recording** of it — takes, cuts, annotations and all — and every recording is listed with its length, its state and the profile it was made under. Nothing about recording, cutting, exporting or publishing changes: the app is always in a project, and **Default** is the one it starts in
- **An update keeps the recording you had** — the previous version's clip becomes a recording of **Default** on the first start, with its takes, cuts, annotations and properties intact. A person who never opens the projects still records, cuts and exports exactly as before; the Default project cannot be renamed or removed, and says why
- **A project's default profile** — a project names the profile its videos start under, set in the project's own dialog. A recording starts under it and **locks** what it declares, exactly as a profile does; a particular recording may use **another profile**, chosen before it captures, and the profile in force is written down with it. Editing or removing a profile later never reaches a recording already made
- **The overview** — a project's overview shows what it is for (its motivation and objectives), its recordings, and how many are finished against the total, so the state of the work is there without counting a list. The description stands in for a recording plan until a structured one exists. A **recording is finished when its clip session ends** — at **New**, on an export or a publish — and reopening one makes it in progress again while its session is open. Beneath the counts, the browser's own storage estimate is read out — what the app is using of what the browser will allow it — so a filling disk is noticed before a take is lost; a browser that gives no estimate shows nothing rather than a wrong number
- **Delete where it lives** — a recording is deleted for good from the overview of the project it is in, behind a confirmation naming it, and its bytes are freed. **Move…** unfiles a recording to **Default**, where it stays whole, or moves it to another project; either way the open recording keeps its project until its session ends
- **The agent organises, never destroys** — an agent can list and read the projects, create one, select the current one, change its description and default profile, move a recording between projects, and move one to Default. Deleting a recording and removing a project are not offered as tools: those need the person's confirmation

## Profiles

- **Profiles — the app's configuration, one set per way of working** — the pill at the right-hand end of the toolbar, wearing the active profile's first letters (**Dem** for DemoClipTV), holds the app's configuration as named **profiles**: **DemoClipTV**, **GraditoneTV**, one for each way of working. A profile **fixes the items it controls**: fix **Add to export** on in DemoClipTV and every clip made under it opens its export with the title card, fix **Logo** to one of the marks DemoClip serves and every one opens with that mark on the card, fix **Annotation colour** and every arrow and text made under it starts in that colour, or fix **Boost the sound** and every clip made under it is brought to a competitive loudness — or kept at the level it was recorded — and none is **yours to change** while the profile is in use, because the row shows its value with a lock whose tooltip names the profile, and nothing to press. An item no profile controls is yours exactly as before; a profile that controls nothing is a **free** profile, and the app's **Empty** is that one — always there, never renamed or removed. A profile's **name** and what it controls are both set in **its own dialog** (the pencil mark on its row) — never in the list, which only selects, creates and removes; the dialog closes by **Done**, Escape or a press outside it. A profile that fixes an **Annotation colour** also wears it as its **badge** — the initials filled with it on its row, and as the toolbar pill's ink and border on the button's own surface — so a way of working is recognised at a glance. The choice belongs to the session, so a profile is **fixed from the moment a take starts recording until the session ends**

## Export

- **Export to WebM or MP4, encoded offline** — WebM is VP9 (VP8 fallback) with Opus audio, MP4 is H.264 with AAC; both are written straight from the decoded frames by WebCodecs: about **10× faster than the clip**, it keeps going while the tab is in the background, and the file carries a duration and cue points, so any player can scrub it. A **sound-only clip** exports the sound alone, in the same two containers, with no video track — so an audio export comes back in through **Import** like any other file; a video service needs a picture, and is offered only for a clip that keeps one
- **Sound boost, on by default** — the clip's sound is brought to a competitive loudness — about **−16 LUFS**, the level a player expects of speech — with a peak ceiling, so a recording made with the microphone a little far still plays at the right level. It is one gain, measured from the clip's kept sound and applied to the sound as it is heard in the review and written in every file (WebM, MP4, a publish, and a sound-only file alike); the recording itself is never changed, so it is **Boost the sound** in the clip's settings — off gives the take back exactly as it was recorded — and a **profile may fix it** on or off. No compression, no noise removal and no mono fold-down: the loudness alone
- **Export progress on the timeline** — the playhead travels across the clip as it is written, with the frames shown on the preview and how much of the clip is left to write counted down; nothing runs off a bottom bar

## A clip that leaves and comes back

- **A clip that leaves and comes back** — **Export ▾ → WebM for editing** writes the picture alone and a **sidecar** beside it holding the annotations as data, so the clip can be worked on by anything on the machine — ffmpeg, an editor, another encoder — and brought back with **Import** (the video and the sidecar chosen together) with its annotations whole: they return as annotations of their own, over the stretch the file was given, ready to be moved again. The plain downloads are untouched by it: a WebM or MP4 file still holds exactly the cuts and the annotations the person watched

## Publish

- **Publish to YouTube** — from the same Export menu, the kept clip goes to your own YouTube channel: sign in with Google once in that window — Google asks which account, and then which channel, your own or a brand account, so the channel is chosen there and nowhere else — set the title, the description and who may see it — **private** unless you say otherwise — and upload. The app says how far it has got, from writing the video to uploading it, and offers the video's own link as soon as YouTube has taken it — the video is on your channel then, and YouTube finishes processing it just after. Nothing is uploaded before you have signed in **and** pressed Upload, no token is kept anywhere, nothing about your account is read, and a clip too long for the account is refused before a frame is written

## Being driven by an agent

- **Drive it from an agent** — a local MCP server (`mcp/`, shipped with the app, on npm as `democlip-mcp`) opens the app in a browser of its own and offers an agent the app's own operations: read the clip, cut, annotate, place the picture on your clipboard, change a property, remove, undo, seek, record a take, bring a video file in as a take, export, publish, and organise the projects and the profiles a recording is made under — a profile being a named set of the clip's own properties it fixes (the logo, the annotation colour, the title card, the export resolution, the sound boost), so a project's recordings open with the same brand. You record exactly as always — the picker is yours, and what is *shared* is chosen by you — and every change the agent makes lands in that window as you watch, is announced to the app's views, and is taken back by the app's **one undo**, whoever made it. There is no tool that runs code in the page, no second editor, and the recording is never re-encoded: an agent's change is the same kind of change a person's is. The server serves the app's own build on loopback and refuses to drive a page of another release, and an export is taken from the browser's own download — the same file you would have got

## The app

- **Speaks your language** — the app reads the language your browser or operating system declares when it loads and shows every word in it — **English** and **Spanish** in this first version — with no setting to find: change the browser's language and reload, and the app follows. English is the source and the fallback, so a language the app does not yet speak still reads whole; adding one is a new catalogue and nothing else
- **Zero dependencies on your machine** — runs entirely in the browser via `getDisplayMedia`
- **Light / dark mode** — full CSS variable theming, persisted to `localStorage`
- **PWA** — installable as a desktop app, offline-capable for core recording and export
- **Version on screen** — the deployed build's version sits beside the logo (in the help panel too), and when a newer build has been deployed the header offers a reload; every merge to `main` bumps it, tags it and deploys — see [VERSIONING.md](VERSIONING.md)
- **100% offline mode** — recording, playback and export happen entirely in your browser, and nothing is uploaded or stored on a server. Publishing a clip — to YouTube or Bluesky — is optional, and only happens when you ask
