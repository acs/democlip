How to use

DemoClip · Quick Reference ·

1. Choose what to record

Nothing is captured until you turn a source on. Press Screen to pick a screen, window or tab (full screen is preselected) and tick Share tab audio / Share system audio for sound — the browser decides whether that box is there and whether it starts ticked, so it is worth a look every time. The source buttons on the left are toggles: turning the screen off lets the share go, and nothing reaches for the screen again until you turn it back on, while Webcam gives a camera bubble over the screen — or the whole picture on its own when the screen is off. If the device has more than one camera, a picker beside Webcam chooses which one. Mic adds your voice: its level shows in the little meter and is mixed with the tab audio, and on its own it records a sound-only clip — no picture at all, reviewed as a waveform. Webcam and Mic are greyed out if there is no device, and all three are out while a take is being set up or records. Press Import to bring a WebM or MP4 file already on your machine in as a take — a video, or a sound-only file — it joins the clip to cut and export like a recording, and nothing is re-recorded. A clip that left through Export ▾ → WebM for editing left a .json beside the video: choose both at once and the annotations come back with the clip. New begins a new clip.

2. Hit record

Press the crimson button in the middle of the bar to start recording — it starts at once. Press again to stop. Press it once more after a recording and the new take is added to the end of the clip, as many times as you like. The share is kept between takes — so they all come from the same tab, window or screen, and there is no second picker. The timeline is there while you record, filling in with the picture and the sound as they are captured, and the recording's duration counts along on it; it becomes the timeline you cut on when you stop. The red box over the preview shows exactly what will be recorded: it covers the whole screen by default, drag to pick a smaller region, and click to go back to the full screen. Everything exports as WebM, a small sharp video for PRs, docs and chat, or as the MP4 that plays anywhere.

3. Stop and cut

Press ▶ (or P) to play the clip back with sound. Click anywhere on the timeline to bring the playhead there — it holds that frame rather than playing, so you can find the exact moment. To cut a part out: click where it starts and click where it ends, then press the scissors — or just click inside the shaded section — and it leaves the timeline, which closes up over it. To point at something instead, press a kind in the annotate options — the column of controls on the picture, under the clip's settings pencil. The arrow is drawn across the preview to point at a detail; the image puts a picture over the picture — choose a file, paste one, or drop one on the preview — and its corners resize it (hold Shift to stretch it freely); the text types a line, and its corners make it bigger or smaller; the magnifier is dragged from the detail outward and shows that detail enlarged inside a round lens, dimming the rest of the picture, with its rim's colour and its zoom in its settings. With a stretch selected on the timeline the annotation is shown over it; with nothing selected it starts at the playhead for a couple of seconds. Either way, open its settings to set how long it lasts — its duration, a seconds value — beside its colour. Click an annotation on the picture to move, aim or resize it, click its mark on the timeline lane to jump to it (a picked annotation's stretch is drawn the whole way along the lane), copy it and paste a new one where the playhead is (⌘/Ctrl+C, ⌘/Ctrl+V). With an annotation picked, the controls that appear on it do the same without a keyboard: the copy puts another like it beside it, the × takes it away, and the settings control changes what it is made of — its colour, how many seconds it is shown (its duration), and for a text its words, typed in the app's own dialog rather than a browser prompt. The undo on the section drops the selection, and the undo in the header brings back whatever you last did, a cut or an annotation. Drag a mark or the shaded section to move it (it resizes if you grab it near an edge), and the frame under it follows as you drag. ← / → nudge the bar a frame at a time (hold Shift for a second), so the cut can land on an exact frame.

4. Export and publish

Give the clip a title and a description from the settings control in the corner of the preview (the pencil over the picture) — what it says about itself, and what publishing fills its fields from. The same control holds the clip's own size (derived from the takes) and the Export resolution: the size the file is finally written at, Clip size by default, or one of the app's own levels (1080p, 720p, …). It is the clip size scaled, its shape kept, so a smaller resolution is a smaller file of the same clip with the review left alone — and the size that file will come to sits under the Export button, so you can watch it change as you choose — and a profile may fix it. Switch on Add to export there and every export — a file or a publish — opens with a 2-second card: the clip's first frame, with those two lines over it and the date and the app's name under them. A profile — the pill at the right-hand end of the header, wearing the active profile's first letters — can fix that switch instead: set it in a profile and every clip made under it is set that way, with the switch not yours to move while that profile is in use — the row shows the value with a lock whose tooltip names the profile. Press the pill to choose a profile, create one or remove one; the pencil mark on a profile's row opens that profile — its name and what it controls. The clip can also wear a logo: set Logo to the name of one of the marks DemoClip ships and every export — a file or a publish — opens with it on the card, above your words. The choice is fixed from the moment you start recording until the session ends, and the app always has an Empty that controls nothing, so nothing changes if you never use them. Then hit Export to choose where the clip goes: a WebM or MP4 file to download, Bluesky to publish it as a video post (sign in with your handle first), or YouTube to upload it to your own channel (sign in with Google). A sound-only clip exports the sound alone — no video track — and its Bluesky and YouTube entries are out, because a video service takes a video. Either file is encoded faster than real time — the timeline counts down how much is left, and the preview shows the frames as they are written, so you can watch it happen without waiting for it.

5. Install as a desktop app

Chrome and Edge show an install prompt (⊕) in the address bar. Click it to run DemoClip as a standalone app with no browser chrome, working offline for WebM recordings.

Everything happens locally in your browser — your recordings and exports are never sent anywhere unless you choose to publish a clip to a video service.
