How to use

DemoClip · Quick Reference ·

1
Choose what to record
Nothing is captured until you turn a source on. Press Screen to pick a screen, window or tab (full screen is preselected) and tick Share tab audio / Share system audio for sound — the browser decides whether that box is there and whether it starts ticked, so it is worth a look every time. The source buttons on the left are toggles: turning the screen off lets the share go, and nothing reaches for the screen again until you turn it back on, while Webcam gives a camera bubble over the screen — or the whole picture on its own when the screen is off. If the device has more than one camera, a picker beside Webcam chooses which one. Mic adds your voice: its level shows in the little meter and is mixed with the tab audio. Webcam and Mic are greyed out if there is no device, and all three are out while a take is being set up or records. Press Import to bring a WebM or MP4 file already on your machine in as a take — it joins the clip to cut, annotate and export like a recording, and nothing is re-recorded. A clip that left through Export ▾ → WebM for editing left a .json beside the video: choose both at once and the annotations come back with the clip. New begins a new clip.
2
Hit record
Press the crimson button in the middle of the bar to start recording — it starts at once. Press again to stop. Press it once more after a recording and the new take is added to the end of the clip, as many times as you like. The share is kept between takes — so they all come from the same tab, window or screen, and there is no second picker. The timeline is there while you record, filling in with the picture and the sound as they are captured, and the recording's duration counts along on it; it becomes the timeline you cut on when you stop. The red box over the preview shows exactly what will be recorded: it covers the whole screen by default, drag to pick a smaller region, and click to go back to the full screen. Everything exports as WebM, a small sharp video for PRs, docs and chat, or as the MP4 that plays anywhere.
3
Stop and cut
Press ▶ (or P) to play the clip back with sound. Click anywhere on the timeline to bring the playhead there — it holds that frame rather than playing, so you can find the exact moment. To cut a part out: click where it starts and click where it ends, then press the scissors — or just click inside the shaded section — and it leaves the timeline, which closes up over it. To point at something instead, press the arrow on the section and drag across the preview: the arrow shows over that stretch in playback and in the file you export. Press the image button beside it to put a picture over the stretch instead — choose a file, paste one, or drop one on the preview — then drag its corners to resize it (hold Shift to stretch it freely). Press the text button to type a line over the stretch, and drag its corners to make it bigger or smaller. Click an annotation on the picture to move, aim or resize it, click its mark on the timeline lane to jump to it, copy it and paste a new one where the playhead is (⌘/Ctrl+C, ⌘/Ctrl+V). With an annotation picked, the controls that appear on it do the same without a keyboard: the copy puts another like it beside it, the × takes it away, and the settings control changes what it is made of — its colour, and for a text its words, typed in the app's own dialog rather than a browser prompt. The undo on the section drops the selection, and the undo in the header brings back whatever you last did, a cut or an annotation. Drag a mark or the shaded section to move it (it resizes if you grab it near an edge), and the frame under it follows as you drag. ← / → nudge the bar a frame at a time (hold Shift for a second), so the cut can land on an exact frame. The estimated file size updates live.
4
Export and publish
Give the clip a title and a description from the settings control in the corner of the preview (the three sliders over the picture) — what it says about itself, and what publishing fills its fields from. Then hit Export to choose where the clip goes: a WebM or MP4 file to download, Bluesky to publish it as a video post (sign in with your handle first), or YouTube to upload it to your own channel (sign in with Google). Either file is encoded faster than real time — the timeline counts down how much is left, and the preview shows the frames as they are written, so you can watch it happen without waiting for it.
5
Install as a desktop app
Chrome and Edge show an install prompt (⊕) in the address bar. Click it to run DemoClip as a standalone app with no browser chrome, working offline for WebM recordings.

Everything happens locally in your browser — your recordings and exports are never sent anywhere unless you choose to publish a clip to a video service.

Publish to Bluesky

Sign in with your handle — your server asks you to approve, then brings you back here. The page reloads on the way back, and your recording comes back with it; you only do this once.

Bluesky takes up to 10 minutes and 300 MB, and re-encodes at about 1920 wide — a wider screen is scaled down, and small text with it. Record a region (the red box) around the window you are filming and its text stays full size.

Upload to YouTube

A default YouTube account takes videos up to 15 minutes. The video is on your channel as soon as it is uploaded, and YouTube finishes processing it just after — so it may take a moment to play. The app is not allowed to read your channel, so it cannot watch that part.
